# Editorial guide

## Voice

Write as a calm, precise independent educational resource. Address the reader as “you.” Use familiar words, define technical terms at first use, and treat adult and younger readers respectfully. Do not invent teaching credentials, student stories, personal trials, or endorsement.

Lead with the answer or activity. Remove throat-clearing, repeated summaries, exaggerated claims, generic encouragement, and search-engine filler. Encourage through an achievable action and useful feedback.

| Avoid | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| Master flawless articulation with this ultimate exercise. | Practice starting four notes evenly. |
| Practice your scales regularly. | Choose a familiar scale. Play slowly enough to keep the rhythm even, then identify the transition that needs attention. |
| A bigger mouthpiece is the next step as you improve. | Explain fit and playing needs; do not present mouthpiece sizes as achievement levels. |
| Always use this exact embouchure. | Explain the goal, show a reviewed example, and identify where individual guidance is needed. |

## Page length and consumption

Use the shortest explanation that enables the task. Working targets, not quotas: quick references 150–400 words; focused lessons 350–750; deeper guides 800–1,400 when necessary. Split a page when it contains separate learning goals, not merely when it passes a word count.

Put the useful action near the top. Use short paragraphs, numbered procedures, and comparison tables where helpful. Keep optional theory below the exercise. Do not hide prerequisites or important limitations in collapsible sections. Avoid forcing identical headings onto every page.

Lesson essentials: outcome, prerequisites, steps, what to notice, an adjustment if it does not work, and a next step. A reference may need only a direct answer, table, scope, and a worked example. A troubleshooting page should separate observable symptoms from possible causes; do not diagnose the reader remotely.

## Musical precision

- Specify written versus concert pitch. Default trumpet exercises to written pitch for B-flat trumpet and state that convention.
- When naming exact pitches, define the octave convention or give staff positions. A note letter alone is not an exact pitch.
- Specify rhythm, tempo when relevant, repetitions, and rest or adaptation instructions. Tempo and repetition suggestions are starting options, not universal prescriptions.
- Define what an acceptable attempt sounds like. A metronome or tuner reading alone is not a complete assessment of musical quality.
- Connect exercises to music. Do not equate level with range or speed.
- Distinguish an established fact, a cited teaching approach, and an original suggested activity.
- Do not prescribe pushing through pain or fatigue. Technique concerns requiring observation should be flagged for a qualified teacher. Medical advice belongs outside this site's lesson scope.

## Sources and originality

Consult primary material where possible: instrument manuals for care; attributed educators for teaching methods; publishers and institutions for repertoire or audition requirements. Open the actual source, confirm it supports the specific claim, and record it in the source ledger. Search snippets are not verification.

Manufacturer material supports its documented instrument instructions; marketing is not independent evidence of superiority. Seek additional perspectives for contested technique and broad product comparisons. Do not treat multiple pages from the same publisher as independent corroboration.

Put reader-useful citations near the supported passage or in a concise further-reading section. Keep detailed claim mappings in the internal ledger. Never invent sources, dates, quotes, testing, or reviewer sign-off. Original exercises should be labeled as editorial suggestions, not proven methods.

Link to licensed recordings and scores. Do not reproduce method-book exercises, arrangements, charts, photographs, or recordings without checking rights. Public-domain status of a composition does not automatically cover its edition or recording. Prefer original notation and demonstrations with recorded permission.

## Media and accessibility

Add audio when hearing the target is essential and a diagram when spatial information is essential. Supply captions/transcripts for instructional video, text descriptions for notation, useful image alt text, and controls without autoplay. Do not rely only on color. Credit assets and record their rights.

An unavailable essential demonstration is a publication blocker, not a placeholder to show readers. Optional media may be deferred if the page works without it.

## Editorial review

Review separately for accuracy, teachability, and voice. Confirm prerequisite order, unambiguous instructions, original/cleared assets, usable source links, accessible presentation, and a functioning next step. Ask whether a reader can actually do something after reading.

Record remaining issues and who reviewed what. “AI editorial review complete” does not mean “teacher reviewed.” Initial physical technique, range/endurance instruction, disputed pedagogy, and specialist performance advice need knowledgeable human review before publication under this proposed workflow. Routine factual references can complete source and editorial review without implying human teaching validation.
