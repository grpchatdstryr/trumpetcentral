# Curriculum map

These are proposed modules, not claims of a standardized grading system. A module may become several focused pages after its brief is written. IDs remain stable even if titles change. Assess readiness by the stated task, with adaptable pacing and no deadline for advancement.

## Foundation: begin making music

| ID | Module | Prerequisites | Observable outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B01 | Meet and handle the trumpet | None | Identify essential parts; handle and put away the instrument with care |
| B02 | First comfortable sound | B01 | Attempt a repeatable sound using a reviewed setup demonstration; recognize when individual help is needed |
| B03 | Repeat and listen to a note | B02 | Repeat a comfortable note and describe its start, duration, and ending |
| B04 | Pulse, notes, and rests | None; B03 for playing | Clap/count a short pattern, then play it on a familiar note |
| B05 | Read your first notes | B04, R01 | Match a small set of written notes to names and staff locations |
| B06 | Change notes with valves | B03, B05, R02 | Play a short reviewed sequence of familiar pitches with coordinated valve changes |
| B07 | Play an easy musical phrase | B04–B06 | Play a short original or cleared melody with rests and a planned ending |
| B08 | Build a repeatable practice habit | B07, P01 | Select one goal, practice it, play music, and record the next step |

B04 includes a no-instrument entry route. Avoid making note reading a prerequisite for the first sound. Pair B02/B03 with essential demonstrations before release.

## Advanced foundations and specialist paths

Shared advanced work: A01 listening and self-assessment (I01, I04, I05); A02 coordinated technique across musical demands (I02, I03, with reviewed individualized workload guidance); A03 performance preparation (I05, I06). Outcomes: identify a musical issue from a recording, select a relevant activity, and evaluate a performance against its musical goals. No universal range or speed threshold.

| Path | Entry guidance | Suggested sequence | Culminating task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Classical | I05–I06; repertoire selected for current ability | Style/listening → solo study → ensemble context → excerpts → instrument/transposition needs | Prepare a short program with stylistic reasons for phrasing choices |
| Jazz and improvisation | Begin rhythm/listening activities from B07; reading-heavy work follows I02 | Listening and feel → learn a melody by ear → short improvisation → blues/form → chord tones → transcription → ensemble interaction | Improvise coherently over an appropriate form and explain a listening influence |
| Ensembles | B07 for accessible groups; assess each part | Following cues → blending/balance → part preparation → rehearsal etiquette → varied settings | Prepare a suitable part and respond to other players in rehearsal |
| Auditions | I06, A01, A03; institution-specific demands | Verify requirements → repertoire plan → recorded feedback → mock audition → logistics | Deliver a prepared mock audition matching the actual requirements |
| Professional preparation | Advanced proficiency appropriate to the work | Reliability and preparation → varied styles → portfolio → collaboration → business-resource literacy | Assemble an accurate portfolio and a practical preparation plan for a defined opportunity |

Professional preparation is a path of resources and tasks, not a promise of employment. Link to appropriate specialists for legal, tax, or health questions. Confirm current audition requirements at the issuing institution before presenting them.

## Expansion backlog

Broaden reference to keys, intervals, transposition, trumpet family, mutes, and terminology. Add reviewed troubleshooting for inconsistent attacks, rhythm, pitch, and difficult transitions. Expand listening across traditions and artists; explain what to listen for rather than ranking players. Develop repertoire guides by musical demands, with edition/recording rights recorded.

Before drafting a specialist module, create its own brief, sources, prerequisite links, review requirements, and media plan. The table above defines coverage; it does not authorize treating a short overview as a complete specialist course.
