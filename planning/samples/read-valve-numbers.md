# Read trumpet valve numbers

For a standard three-piston-valve trumpet, valve **1** is closest to the mouthpiece, **2** is in the middle, and **3** is closest to the bell. They are normally operated by the right index, middle, and ring fingers respectively.

Numbers tell you which valves to press. “Open” means no valves are pressed.

| Marking | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Open or 0 | Leave all three valves up |
| 1 | Press only valve 1 |
| 2 | Press only valve 2 |
| 1–2 | Press valves 1 and 2 together |
| 1–3 | Press valves 1 and 3 together |
| 2–3 | Press valves 2 and 3 together |
| 1–2–3 | Press all three valves together |

The dash joins valve numbers; it does not mean to press them in sequence. Check each chart's legend because symbols vary. Yamaha provides a [fingering chart and valve-number legend](https://www.yamaha.com/en/musical_instrument_guide/common/images/trumpet/fingering.pdf).

## A fingering is not a unique pitch

Several notes share a fingering. Open valves, for example, can produce more than one note. A fingering chart must show the specific note as well as the valve combination. Yamaha explains this relationship in its [guide to trumpet sound and valve operation](https://www.yamaha.com/en/musical_instrument_guide/trumpet/mechanism/mechanism002.html).

## Check your understanding

With the trumpet resting securely in your hands, try “open,” then “1,” then “1–2.” Which valves stay up each time? You do not need to blow into the instrument to practice reading these markings.
