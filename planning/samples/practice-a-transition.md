# Practice a difficult transition

When the same spot interrupts a passage, give that transition its own practice time. Work on a small section, then check whether the improvement carries back into the phrase.

Use music you can mostly play already. This activity assumes you can read its notes and rhythms and that the passage sits within your comfortable playing range.

## Identify what happens

Play the passage once at a manageable pace, or listen to a recent recording. Describe the problem in observable terms: “The second note arrives late,” “I hesitate at the accidental,” or “The rhythm changes when I move my fingers.”

Choose one issue. If you are unsure of a note or rhythm, work that out before repeating the passage.

## Make the section small

Select a few notes containing the transition. Include the note before the difficulty and the note after it, so you practice entering and leaving the spot.

For a rhythm problem, count or clap that section first. For a fingering hesitation, check the notes and move through their fingerings slowly before playing. These checks help separate tasks; they do not replace listening to the played result.

## Test one change

1. Choose a tempo at which you can attempt the section without rushing. There is no required starting speed.
2. Play the section once, then pause. Did the specific issue improve?
3. If it did not, make the section simpler or the tempo more manageable. Change one thing at a time.
4. If it did, try another attempt after a break. Look for a repeatable result, including the intended rhythm and articulation.

If the same mistake continues, stop the loop and reconsider what is causing the difficulty. More repetitions are useful only when you know what you are trying to change.

## Put it back in the phrase

Add a little music before and after the section. Keep the same manageable tempo. Then try the full phrase.

If the problem returns, check what changed: the approach, breath placement, rhythm, or attention. Return to a section large enough to include that context.

Increase tempo only when the passage remains coordinated. Use a small increase that lets you hear whether the result changes; a particular metronome number is not the goal.

## Leave a useful note for tomorrow

Write down the location, the issue, and the next action. For example: “Measure 8: hesitation at the accidental. Start with the last two notes of measure 7 and continue through measure 8.”

This procedure is an original practice suggestion, not a guaranteed learning schedule. Finish by playing something manageable that lets you concentrate on the musical phrase.
